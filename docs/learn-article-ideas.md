## Planned Articles

### From User
1. **Evolutionary timeline** — When major plant, fungal, and lichen lineages diverged (ferns 360mya, conifers 300mya, flowering plants 130mya, lichens as ancient symbioses). Visual/interactive timeline format.
2. **Spring ephemerals** — The wildflowers that race to bloom before the canopy closes: trout lily, bloodroot, trillium, spring beauty. Ties into phenology.
3. **Pollinator scouting in spring** — How to observe pollinators: mining bees, bumblebee queens emerging, early butterflies (mourning cloaks, spring azures), hoverflies. Practical field guide style.
4. **Parasitic plants and fungi** — The "dark side" of nature: Indian pipe, beechdrops, dodder (plants); honey mushroom, cordyceps, chaga (fungi).
5. **Trees vs shrubs** — The surprisingly tricky distinction. Multi-stemmed vs single trunk, height thresholds, species that can be either (witch hazel, serviceberry).

### Additional Ideas
6. **Mycorrhizal networks** — The "wood wide web." How 90% of plants partner with fungi underground. Connects plants and fungi groups.
7. **Lichen: not a single organism** — Algae + fungus symbiosis, sometimes with cyanobacteria. The 3 growth forms (crustose, foliose, fruticose).
8. **Invasive species of New England** — Bittersweet, Japanese knotweed, garlic mustard, emerald ash borer. Practical identification of threats.
9. **Reading tree bark** — How to ID trees in winter without leaves. Birches, beech, shagbark hickory, sycamore distinctive bark patterns.
10. **Vernal pools** — Temporary spring ponds critical for spotted salamanders, wood frogs, fairy shrimp. Ties together amphibians, insects, and habitat.
11. **Edible vs toxic lookalikes** — Chanterelle vs jack-o-lantern, wild carrot vs poison hemlock, chicken of the woods. High practical value.
12. **Why leaves change color** — The chemistry of fall foliage. Chlorophyll, carotenoids, anthocyanins. Iconic New England topic.
